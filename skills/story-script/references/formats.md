# Format grammars

Each section gives the spine, the timing (house defaults, tune by eye and by retention data), the rules that keep it a story, and what usually goes wrong. Timings in seconds unless marked as frames.

## Ad

**Spine:** hook, problem, product, proof, CTA. It maps onto YouTube's published ABCD guidance (Attract, Brand, Connect, Direct) and onto motion-pack's `templates/ad`.

| block | job | 15 s | 30 s | 45 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | stop the scroll with the stake | 0-2.5 | 0-3 | 0-3 |
| Problem | one pain, felt by a person | 2.5-5 | 3-8 | 3-11 |
| Product | the reveal answers the pain; one benefit per shot | 5-9 | 8-16 | 11-24 |
| Proof | a real demonstration, a real number with a source, a real screen | 9-12.5 | 16-25 | 24-37 |
| CTA | one specific action; urgency only if true | 12.5-15 | 25-30 | 37-45 |

- **One pain, not three.** Three pains split the viewer's sympathy and triple the shots.
- **The reveal needs a hold** (3-4 s hero shot) even when the rest cuts every 1.2-2 s.
- **Brand cue:** paid YouTube wants the brand inside 5 s (ABCD); organic feeds tolerate it after the hook, before about 7 s. motion-pack checks this as `brandCueBy`.
- **Objective changes the body:** awareness adds music, people and repeated brand touches; consideration adds use-in-context; action makes the product the hero and adds the offer.
- **A 6 s bumper** is hook, product and logo: 0-2, 2-4.5, 4.5-6.
- **Goes wrong when:** the proof is a vague claim ("loved by teams"), the problem is abstract, or the CTA asks for two things.

## Launch film

**Spine:** a story with one protagonist (a person or a small firm), one goal and one clock, carrying the classic launch beats (hook, tease, reveal, story-tied montage, end card).

- **Say what it is by about 3 s and show the brand by about 5 s.** A demonstration (unplug it, it still works) beats any claim.
- **Every scene moves the one matter forward and carries a time.** The reveal resolves the story; the button is wit (a last line that lands with a smile), and nothing follows the button except the logo.
- **Show breadth in a form the eye cannot leave:** parallel lanes, an escalation, or features triggered by the story. A one-by-one tour of capabilities is where viewers leave.
- **A visual law can carry the promise.** Example: "light comes in; nothing goes out": a window is open only while the hero is tempted to send a file away, and once it is latched no paper moves toward the glass again. The idea is never said; it is staged.
- **Author the lead aspect natively** and re-lay out the others; a 9:16 film stretched into 16:9 leaves empty quarters.
- **Goes wrong when:** it becomes a feature parade, it grows by adding every note (more supers, more languages, a voice-over nobody asked for), or it relies on abstract "AI" imagery. When it is rejected, subtract.

## Short film

**Spine** (percent of runtime): cold open image 0-8% · world and want 8-25% · one obstacle 25-50% · **turn 50-60%** · consequence 60-85% · resolution image 85-95% · credit tag 95-100% (6 s or less). A 60 s piece collapses to hook, want, turn, button.

- **One idea, one turn.** Under about 5 minutes there is room for one change. Shapes that fit: a moment of change, an ironic reversal (plant the misdirection fast), a visual metaphor, a build-up.
- **The cold open is a question, not exposition.**
- **Scale ladder:** wide to establish, medium to connect, close to feel, insert for detail. Tighten as emotion rises; break the pattern on the turn (a sudden wide or a sudden detail). No more than 3 same-scale shots in a row.
- **Hard cuts by default.** A dissolve means time passed; J and L cuts bridge scenes; film burns and light leaks only for memory or a tonal break.
- **Sound first:** room tone under every scene; the score enters on emotion, not on action; 200-400 ms of silence before the turn.
- **A bookend:** one visual idea (a prop, a colour, a shape) appears early and returns transformed at the end.
- **The product's arc in a film:** planted in the first shot and forgotten, rediscovered at the turn, used through screens and geography, acknowledged by a glance, and present in the last image. It never moves, flashes or speaks to get attention.
- **Dramatic irony works:** the audience sees the product arrive; the characters only see the results. One character can be the bridge who knows.
- **Goes wrong when:** the first 20 s are mood with no conflict (fine for long-form, fatal in a vertical feed: cut a flash-forward cold open for feeds), or the payoff is a generic celebration instead of the stake resolved.

## Character spot (wordless)

**The grid** (18 s master at 30 fps; a 15 s cut drops attempt 2; a 20 s cut adds a callback tag after the payoff):

| beat | seconds | job |
|---|---|---|
| HOOK | 0-1 | the gag already moving on frame 0; the first reaction; an optional chip of at most three words |
| SETUP | 1-5 | attempt 1 (small), attempt 2 (bigger): the rule of three |
| ESC | 5-7.5 | attempt 3; the problem peaks |
| FREEZE | 7.5-8.0 | everything holds; only one small look moves; the music drops to a drone, then true silence |
| TURN | 8.0 | one product verb; the light flips from problem to solved |
| PAYOFF | 9-14 | the feature as a visible state change, then the reaction |
| LINE | 14.0 | one end line, six words or fewer, naming the feature |
| CARD | 16.0-18.0 | logo, line, URL, fine print; the sonic sting |

**Timing laws:** the protagonist hurries and the product never does; that contrast in timing (not in easing) is the comic engine. One hero and one motion per beat. Hold at least half a second after every key action. The camera never moves during a drop, a snap or a stamp.

**World rules** (write your own in the series bible; these held up):
1. The product never speaks and never hurries. It moves only to solve, with one verb.
2. Problems are objects (a paper tower, a dial, a cloud), never cruel people.
3. Nobody is hurt, shamed or fired; the worst outcome is mild embarrassment.
4. Light tells the state; a viewer who sees it flip once understands it forever.
5. Every spot ends the same way: state change, reaction, line.

**Gag mechanics** (one main, at most one secondary): rule of three, scale gag, race the clock, caught red-handed, deadpan refusal, misdirection, the silence beat, callback. A familiar format (a queue, a quiz show, a board meeting) gives the setup for free.

**A tiny vocabulary:** six to ten poses plus eye states and one signature gesture. Faces are shape swaps, not morphs, so they read at thumbnail size. Sound gives the character a voice without words (pitched blips or babble) and the product a signature (a chord, a ding, a sting that doubles as a notification tone).

**Lessons from the classic wordless mascot campaigns:** one service per film, no dialogue, a minimal neutral world and a gag that resolves on the service made them memorable and cheap enough to make many. What went wrong was also consistent: the characters were remembered better than the services, the format drifted from "one service, one gag" into spin-offs and contests, too many brand faces competed, and one gag that played with self-harm was banned. So: the payoff is the feature, the end line names it, the product is in every spot and the logo lands last, and no gag depends on harm.

**Tests before posting:** a muted test (3 of 5 people can say what happened) and a feature-recall test (3 of 5 can name the feature).

## Chat skit

A comedy told in chat bubbles (a text thread, a group chat), music-led, with a sound per bubble.

| part | rule |
|---|---|
| Hook title | the stake in searchable words: a client, money, a deadline, a secret |
| First bubble | the stake itself, on frame 0. Never a name, a greeting or a divider |
| Escalation | three beats, each worse than the last |
| Twist punchline | the reversal; hard-cut the music on it |
| Callback | one line after the music cut that rewards regulars (a running number, a time, a phrase) |
| Close | "send this to ..." naming a person the viewer knows |

- **Pay off early.** Feed viewers decide in the first seconds, so a punchline at 7-8 s reaches far more of them than one at 14 s. Aim for 15 s or less where the joke allows.
- **One idea per bubble**, short enough to read in a beat. A reacting face or character on frame 0, inside the safe area, helps the hook.
- **Stakes beat banter.** Relatable office pain with something at stake (a client's payment, a confidential file, a late-evening "one small thing") travels; good-morning banter does not.
- **Keep the product out of the video.** Claims live in the description. If the product appears, it is a prop inside the human conflict.
- **No real company names**, even as an SMS sender. Invent them.

## Explainer

**Spine:** a question the viewer has · the wrong model they hold · the right model, shown as one diagram or demonstration · proof (real) · so-what (what to do now).

- One idea per explainer. If it needs two diagrams, it is two explainers.
- Draw the diagram with a diagram or math animation tool, then finish sound, type and pacing in motion-pack.
- Every number has a source; placeholder data is labelled until it is replaced.
- **Goes wrong when:** it opens with a definition instead of the question, or shows the full diagram at once instead of building it.

## Reel or hook test

**Spine:** hook (0-3 s) · why-stay promise or open loop (3-6 s) · 2-4 beats, each with a micro-payoff and introduced by a visible change · the best payoff in the final 20% · a loop seam or a CTA.

- **Loops:** state-return (the last frame equals the first), reveal-to-hook (the last line completes the first) or ping-pong. On platforms that count replays as views, a seamless loop is worth views directly.
- **A send-worthy beat** in the final third that names an audience.
- **Trim a dead ending.** If retention dips at the very end, cut the last 1-2 s.

## Series formats

- **One feature per episode** tells a whole product through a series; no single episode carries the suite.
- **Build the bible first** (`templates.md` section 5): grammar, cast, world rules, callbacks, labels, banned list.
- **Cheap enough to repeat.** A series only works if each episode costs little; reuse the set, the rig and the sound kit.
- **Review after a season:** renew or retire on purpose, rather than fading by neglect or drifting into spin-offs.
